## Appendix: The Drowned Bell (A Complete First Adventure)

You have the methods. Here is a whole first session built with them, ready to run. Every
template in this book is filled in below, so you can read it as a finished adventure and
as a worked example at the same time. Change any name that does not fit your table. Cross
out anything you do not use. It is built for four new players at level one and runs in a
single evening of about three hours.

Read it once, keep this appendix open beside your screen, and go.

### The pitch, in three sentences

The village of Hollowmere sits on the edge of a flooded quarry. For a hundred years a
bell has hung in the old chapel tower at the water's bottom, silent. Three nights ago it
began to ring on its own, and every night since, someone in the village has walked into
the water in their sleep and not come back.

That is the whole hook. A ringing bell, sleepwalkers drowning, a village that is scared.
The players are strangers passing through who are asked, or paid, to make it stop.

### The opening scene (do not skip this)

Start in the flooded street at dusk, not the tavern. The party arrives as villagers are
tying a rope around a sleepwalking woman before she reaches the water. She is calm, eyes
open, humming a tune in time with a bell only she can hear. A man begs the party for
help. The trouble is already happening on the first line. That is your start.

### The First-Session Checklist, filled in

```
FIRST SESSION: The Drowned Bell        DATE: ____

1 · THE OPENING SCENE
   Where they start: the flooded main street of Hollowmere at dusk
   The trouble already happening: villagers restraining a sleepwalker
   First line I say: "She's humming. Her feet are already in the water."

2 · TONIGHT'S GOAL
   One thing they could do: find why the bell rings and silence it

3 · THREE NPCs
   Elder Maren — the reeve, wants the deaths hidden to avoid panic
   Toma — a boy whose sister already drowned, wants revenge, knows the water
   Sister Vey — a hedge-priest, wants the chapel's sin finally confessed

4 · ONE DANGER
   What can go wrong: the drowned dead rise when the bell rings
   Where/when it hits: at the bottom of the quarry, near the tower

5 · MY SAFETY LINE
   If I freeze, I ask: "What does your character do?"
```

### The village and its people

Hollowmere is thirty houses, a chapel, and a quarry that flooded a lifetime ago. The
people are tired and frightened, and each of the three named NPCs holds one of the three
clues the party needs. Play them with the one-line cards below.

```
NPC CARD — Elder Maren
   Where met: the main street, organizing the rope
   WANT: keep the deaths quiet so the village does not empty out
   TRAIT: answers every question with a question
   VOICE: clipped, official
   OPINION OF PARTY: distrusts (outsiders mean gossip)
   SECRET/HOOK: knows the drowned all shared one ancestor

NPC CARD — Toma
   Where met: at the water's edge, throwing stones at the quarry
   WANT: someone to blame, and to save the next sleeper
   TRAIT: will not look at the water while he talks about it
   VOICE: fast, angry, young
   HOOK: saw his sister walk in; she was humming the bell's tune

NPC CARD — Sister Vey
   Where met: the chapel, praying over a list of names
   WANT: the village to finally admit what happened here
   TRAIT: touches the old bell-rope burn scar on her palm
   VOICE: slow, gentle, certain
   HOOK: knows a bell-ringer was sealed in the tower alive, long ago
```

### The mystery and the three ways in

The key fact the players must reach is simple: a hundred years ago the village sealed a
plague-sick bell-ringer inside the tower to keep the sickness out, and let the quarry
flood over him. His anger is what rings the bell and calls the sleepers to join him. The
players do not need to guess this. Three separate clues each point at it, and finding any
one is enough.

```
ADVENTURE ONE-PAGE — The Drowned Bell

THE PARTY'S GOAL: stop the bell and the drownings

THREE-CLUE RULE (key fact: a ringing bell = a man sealed to drown, calling company)
   Clue path 1: Maren admits the drowned all share one ancestor, the family
                that sealed the tower
   Clue path 2: Toma repeats the tune his sister hummed; Vey knows it as the
                old ringer's hymn
   Clue path 3: church records in the chapel name a ringer "given to the water"
                in the plague year

THE CLOCK (why idling costs them)
   The bell rings again at midnight. Each night, one more villager walks in.
   Tonight it will be Toma unless they act.

PRESSURE MOVES (use if they stall)
   • The bell begins to ring early; a sleeper they know starts humming
   • Maren tries to send them away to protect the village's name

CLIFFHANGER IF YOU SPLIT IT OVER TWO NIGHTS:
   the water in the quarry begins, slowly, to lower on its own
```

### Into the water: the Drowned Bell tower

When the party goes down into the flooded quarry (a diving charm, a held breath and a
Constitution check, or "the water pulls back at midnight and a stair appears," your
call), they enter the tower as a five-room crawl.

```
5-ROOM DUNGEON — The Drowned Bell        THEME: a flooded chapel tower

WHY THE PARTY COMES HERE: to reach and silence the bell

1 · ENTRANCE + GUARDIAN
   The tower door, held shut by the weight of a hundred years of silt.
   Cost to enter: a Strength check, or clear the silt while something
   watches from the dark water.

2 · PUZZLE / ROLEPLAY CHALLENGE
   A ring of drowned villagers, calm and humming, block the stair. They will
   not fight. They will only move if a living person hums the ringer's hymn
   back to them (a clue from Toma or Vey), acknowledging him.

3 · THE SETBACK
   The bell rings. The recently drowned rise as grasping dead (see encounter
   below). The stair begins to flood again; the party has a few rounds.

4 · THE CLIMAX
   The bell-chamber. The ringer's bound spirit hangs in the water, ringing the
   bell with no hands. He wants one thing: to be heard, and freed, or destroyed.

5 · REWARD + TWIST
   Silence the bell and the water stills. The reward is the village's thanks
   and the ringer's old silver seal. The twist: the seal bears Maren's family
   crest. Her ancestors sealed him. She knew.
```

### The one fight, built to the budget

The adventure needs only one real fight, at the setback. Keep it fair for level-one
newcomers with the budget below.

```
ENCOUNTER BALANCE — The Rising Drowned        PARTY: 4 at level 1

BUDGET: standard = 4 threat points
SPEND IT:
   Drowned villager (weak, grasping) x4 ...... ~2 pts   "zombie-lite"
   Bell-called revenant (mid threat) x1 ...... ~2 pts   the loudest sleeper
   Total ≈ 4 pts (matches budget)

DANGER DIAL (turned):
   Terrain: rising water, difficult to move each round
   Reinforcements: one more drowned rises each round the bell still rings
   The way out: silence the bell and every drowned goes still at once

WHAT THEY GET FOR WINNING: the water stills, the village is saved, the seal
```

The design teaches the players the win condition through the fight: attacking the dead is
slow and endless, but the bell is the source. A smart party stops fighting and goes for
the bell. Reward that. If they do not see it, have a drowned villager, in the ringer's
voice, whisper "make it stop" and glance at the bell.

### How it ends, and where it goes next

If the party frees or destroys the ringer, the bell falls silent and the quarry stills.
Give them the village's real gratitude, a warm meal, and the silver seal. Then let the
twist land in its own time: the seal is Maren's family crest, and she has known the truth
all along. That is your hook into session two, already built by the ending of session one,
exactly as Chapter 11 promised. The players will want to know what else the village buried,
and you will run the one-hour prep on wherever they decide to look.

You have run a whole adventure out of five filled-in templates. That is the entire method
of this book, start to finish. Now go build your own.
