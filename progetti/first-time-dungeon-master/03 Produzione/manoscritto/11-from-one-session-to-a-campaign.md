## Chapter 11: From One Session to a Campaign

You ran the first session. The table laughed, someone almost died, and as everyone packed
up a player asked the best question a DM can hear: "So when's next time?" Now you have a
new job, and it is easier than the first one. A campaign is not eleven times the work of a
session. It is one session, then another, connected by a thin thread you tend between
games. This chapter shows you how to tend it without drowning in prep.

The mistake that ends first campaigns is not too little planning. It is too much. New DMs
try to map a year of story, the players ignore the plan in week two, and the DM feels like
a failure and quits. Do the opposite. Plan lightly, react fast, and let the campaign grow
out of what the players actually do.

### Close every session on purpose

The last five minutes of a session set up the next one, so do not let them dribble away.
Do two things before everyone leaves. End on a hook, the cliffhanger you learned in Chapter
9, so the group leaves hungry. Then take one minute to jot what just happened: what the
party did, what they said they would do next, and any thread you left dangling. That note is
tomorrow's prep, half-written, while it is still fresh.

### Prep the next session, not the whole story

Between games, you do the one-hour prep from Chapter 2, aimed at what the players pointed
at when they left. You do not prepare the branches they might take. You prepare the branch
they said they would take, and you keep the improv tables from Chapter 5 ready for the rest.
This is the whole rhythm of running a campaign: a light note at the end of each session, one
hour of focused prep before the next, and trust in your tools for everything you did not see
coming.

### Let the thread thicken slowly

A campaign gains depth by remembering, not by planning. The bandit the players spared in
session two comes back grateful in session six. The offhand rumor about the lake becomes the
next arc because the players kept poking at it. Your job between sessions is to look at your
notes and ask one question: what did the players care about, and how does the world respond?
Follow their attention and the campaign writes itself, one callback at a time. The villain
you planned matters less than the enemy they made by accident.

### Grow as a DM the same way

You will get better session by session, and the fastest way is to keep the same short note
for yourself that you keep for the story. After each game, write one thing that worked and
one thing you would change. Not a review, one line each. Over ten sessions those lines
become your own style guide, earned at the table instead of read in a book. The confident DM
you want to be is only you, a dozen sessions from now, having paid attention.

### At a real table
After session one, Bel spends sixty seconds on a recap: the party freed a prisoner,
promised to warn the next village, and left a bandit alive. Before the next game she runs
the one-hour prep on the village they are heading to, and she rewards their mercy: the
spared bandit turns up as a nervous informant. Ten sessions later, her "plan" is mostly
callbacks to things the players did, stitched together one recap at a time. She never
mapped a year of story. She only kept the note, and the campaign grew itself.

> **At the table:** keep a running "party legend" list where everyone can see it: names
> they've met, places they've been, promises they've made. It saves you from contradicting
> yourself, and it shows the players their choices are remembered, which is the feeling that
> turns a game group into a campaign.

### Carry it forward with two sheets

The templates below close the loop. The recap sheet captures a session in one minute while
it is fresh. The next-session sheet turns that recap into your one-hour prep. Run this loop
and a campaign stops being a mountain and becomes what it should be: the next good night,
and then the one after that.

### The Session Recap and Next-Session Prep Sheets

One pair per session. Printable copies in the download pack (last page).

```
SESSION RECAP (fill in one minute, right after playing)
   Session #: ___   Date: ___
   What the party did: _____________________________
   What they said they'll do next: _________________
   Threads left dangling: __________________________
   New names/places/promises to remember: __________

NEXT-SESSION PREP (fill before you play again)
   Their stated next step points to: _______________
   → run the 1-Hour Prep Worksheet (Chapter 2) on THAT
   One callback to reward their past choices: ______
   One thread to nudge if they stall: ______________
```

That is the whole job, start to finish. You can prep a session in an hour, run the table
with confidence, and turn one night into a campaign. Your first session starts now. Go
roll some dice.
